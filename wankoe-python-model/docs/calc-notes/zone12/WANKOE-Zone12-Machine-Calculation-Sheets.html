Scope and method

This document presents the engineering calculation of the machines of Zone 1.2 of the WANKOE limestone processing line, for independent validation. It follows the same method as the Zone-1 companion document: for each machine, its function, input data, constraints, the engineering model with every symbol defined and its literature source, the numerical application on validated project data, and the computed results. All figures come from the same deterministic flowsheet model (closed mass balances, closed circuits solved by fixed-point iteration on the recirculating stream). The document is self-contained.

Zone 1.2 in the plant

Zone 1 (companion document) produces a 0/20 mm stockpile alongside the KFS kiln stone. Zone 1.2 reclaims this stockpile and converts it into two products: AgLime 0/1.7 mm (agricultural lime, market cap 135 000 t/y) and FeedLime 5–15 mm, the feedstock of Zone 1.3 (drying/milling for feed-grade products, whose firm 40 000 t/y grits contract creates a FeedLime demand of 162 075 t/y). The chain: reclaim feeder BF.5101 (100 t/h, no process calculation — a constant-rate extractor) → double-deck screen SR.5105 (15/5 mm) → the 5–15 mm mid cut goes to the FeedLime stockpile, while the +15 and 0–5 cuts feed the AgLime closed loop: closing screen SR.5115 (1.7 mm) whose oversize is crushed by impactor CR.5107 and returned.

Operating modes. 2A (computed here): mid cut → FeedLime, rest → AgLime loop. 2B: the whole reclaim → FeedLime, loop stopped — this needs no machine model: it is the exact mass identity FeedLime = reclaim. Rain forces 2B, because the 1.7 mm cut is physically impossible on wet fines (the partition collapses); 2B is also selectable in dry weather. 2C: everything loops back to stock (flushing/return mode, no product; not computed).

Sheet 1  ·  SR.5105 — double-deck reclaim screen 15/5 mm

Primary split of the reclaimed 0/20 · feed: BF.5101 · products: +15 and 0–5 (to AgLime loop) · 5–15 = FeedLime

1.1 Function

SR.5105 performs the three-way split that separates the FeedLime product from the AgLime loop feed. Its top deck (15 mm) removes the coarse fraction; its bottom deck (5 mm) isolates the 5–15 mm FeedLime cut from the 0–5 mm fines. Routing depends on the operating mode (§ preamble); in mode 2A the +15 and 0–5 streams merge into the closed loop.

1.3 Model — partition curve (Karra)

A real screen deck gives a particle of size \(x\) a probability \(\rho(x)\) of reporting to the oversize; Karra (1979) [3] describes it by a log-logistic function centred on the corrected cut size:

\(\displaystyle \rho(x)=\frac{1}{1+\left(d_{50c}/x\right)^{s}},\qquad d_{50c}=k_d\,a,\qquad s=\frac{\ln 9}{\ln\!\bigl(1/(1-I)\bigr)}\)
(1.1)

where the sharpness \(s\) follows from the quartile definition \(\rho(d_{25})=0.25\), \(\rho(d_{75})=0.75\) and the imperfection \(I\) is defined by \(d_{25}/d_{75}=1-I\) (derivation in the companion document, Sheet 2). With \(I=0.15\): \(s=13.52\). Flows are computed class by class and mass is conserved exactly. Required deck area follows the VSMA/Fontaine capacity law:

\(\displaystyle Q_b=14\,a^{0.6}\ \ \mathrm{[t/h/m^2]},\qquad A=\frac{U\,f_p}{Q_b\,f_0},\qquad f_p=1.2,\ f_0=0.347\)
(1.2)

The capacity law is a field heuristic flagged for cross-checking (companion document, §2.5 note). Under rain, wet screening loses 25 % capacity — but rain forces mode 2B on this zone anyway (the sizing consequence is shown in §1.5).

1.4 Numerical application

\(\displaystyle Q_{b,\mathrm{top}}=14\times 15^{0.6}=71.1,\qquad A_{\mathrm{top}}=\frac{78.25\times 1.2}{71.1\times 0.347}=3.81\ \mathrm{m^2}\)
(1.3)
\(\displaystyle Q_{b,\mathrm{bot}}=14\times 5^{0.6}=36.8,\qquad A_{\mathrm{bot}}=\frac{31.25\times 1.2}{36.8\times 0.347}=2.94\ \mathrm{m^2}\)
(1.4)

Sheet 2  ·  SR.5115 — closing screen 1.7 mm (AgLime loop)

Closes the AgLime loop · feed: +15 and 0–5 from SR.5105 + CR.5107 recycle · undersize 0/1.7 = AgLime product

2.1 Function

SR.5115 makes the AgLime product cut. Its oversize goes to the impact crusher CR.5107 and returns: screen and crusher form a closed circuit. A structural property of any closed loop follows from mass conservation at stationarity: everything that enters the loop leaves as undersize — the AgLime rate equals the fresh loop feed, independently of the crusher setting, which only governs the circulating load:

\(\displaystyle \dot m_{\mathrm{AgLime}}=\dot m_{\mathrm{loop,in}},\qquad CL=\frac{R}{F}=\frac{1-e}{e}\)
(2.1)
\(R\)
recirculated flow; \(F\) fresh loop feed; \(e\) screen extraction efficiency

2.3 Model and validity

Karra partition (1.1) at \(a=1.7\) mm, \(I=0.15\), \(s=13.52\); area law (1.2). Validity note: Karra's model is established for apertures > 1 mm — the 1.7 mm cut sits near this limit; the partition is retained as the best available model, flagged for the ongoing expert review. Under rain the imperfection collapses and the cut becomes impossible: this is the physical basis of the forced mode-2B switch, not a modelling choice.

Sheet 3  ·  CR.5107 — impact crusher (AgLime loop)

Crushes the +1.7 oversize and returns it to SR.5115 · the heaviest specific comminution duty of the line

3.1 Function and settings

CR.5107 closes the AgLime loop by crushing the +1.7 mm oversize to near-product size in one pass. Per (2.1) its settings do not change the AgLime rate; they set the circulating load and its own power. Settings: rotor speed \(v=40\) m/s, CSS = 1.0 mm — the minimum-circulating-load recommendation of the project's model book (CSS 1.0–1.2 mm, \(v\approx 40\) m/s). Breakage parameters \(A=60\), \(b=0.80\) [H] (calcite drop-weight literature gives softer values, A 62–69, b 1.3–3.0 [6]; kept conservative pending site tests).

3.2 Model — impact fragmentation (JKMRC \(t_{10}\))

A particle struck at peripheral speed \(v\) receives the specific kinetic energy \(\tfrac12 v^2\) (J/kg), i.e. in kWh/t (1 kWh/t = 3600 J/kg):

\(\displaystyle E_{cs}=\frac{v^{2}}{7200},\qquad t_{10}=A\left(1-e^{-b\,E_{cs}}\right),\qquad n=\max\!\left(0.65,\ \left(\tfrac{30}{t_{10}}\right)^{0.30}\right)\)
(3.1)

(Narayanan & Whiten, JKMRC [7]). The product follows the truncated Rosin–Rammler distribution with \(x_{80}=\mathrm{CSS}\) (companion document, Sheet 1, eq. 1.2), and power follows Bond (companion document, eq. 1.3). The loop is solved by fixed-point iteration until the recycle stream converges in flow and size distribution.

3.3 Numerical application

\(\displaystyle E_{cs}=\frac{40^{2}}{7200}=0.2222\ \mathrm{kWh/t},\qquad t_{10}=60\left(1-e^{-0.8\times 0.2222}\right)=9.77\ \%,\qquad n=1.400\)
(3.2)
\(\displaystyle W=10\times 12.54\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{947}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{17\,167}}\right)=3.12\ \mathrm{kWh/t}\)
(3.3)
\(\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{net}}=3.12\times 37.74=117.7\ \mathrm{kW},\qquad P_{\mathrm{inst}}=\frac{117.7}{0.75}=156.9\ \mathrm{kW}\)
(3.4)

Validity: the reduction 17.2 → 0.95 mm (ratio 18) is far beyond Bond's comfort zone (ratios 3–6); \(W=3.12\) kWh/t is the line's heaviest specific duty and its least certain power figure — treat as an order-of-magnitude for motor selection, to be confirmed by the vendor.

4  ·  Zone balance, mode 2B, and weekly production plan

4.1 Mass balance (mode 2A, dry)

\(\displaystyle \underbrace{93.00}_{\text{reclaim}}=\underbrace{47.00}_{\text{FeedLime}}+\underbrace{46.00}_{\text{AgLime}}\ \ \mathrm{t/h\ dry}\qquad(\text{relative gap}<10^{-6})\)
(4.1)

4.2 Mode 2B (rain, or by choice)

The loop stops; the mass identity FeedLime = reclaim holds exactly: 100 t/h wet of 0/20 becomes FeedLime at 100 t/h wet. No machine calculation is involved; SR.5105 runs as a bypass conveyor (its rain sizing is in §1.5). Mode 2B makes FeedLime twice as fast as mode 2A (100 vs 50.5 t/h wet) — the fact the weekly plan exploits.

4.3 Weekly production plan (client planning rule: FeedLime demand drives; AgLime up to its market cap, zero surplus)

Zone 1.3's FeedLime demand is 162 075 t/y = 3 117 t/week, continuous. The AgLime market cap (135 000 t/y) limits mode-2A operation; the FeedLime balance is completed in mode 2B during the 13 rain weeks. Weekly ceiling: 21 × 8-h shifts = 168 h, of which 23.8 h/week are reserved for maintenance → net 144.2 h/week; hours below are clock hours at 80 % availability.

The seasonal FeedLime imbalance is carried by the stockpile: dry weeks overproduce (+420 t/wk to stock), rain weeks underproduce (−1 262 t/wk from stock); the two balance over the year (39 × 420 ≈ 13 × 1 262). The stockpile must therefore hold a seasonal swing of ≈ 16 400 t of FeedLime — a storage-capacity requirement for the yard design.

Planning statement. Zone 1.2 is comfortable: 49.5 % of its hour ceiling serves both the firm-grits-driven FeedLime demand and the full AgLime market, with zero unsellable surplus. Its feasibility is conditional on Zone 1 delivering the 0/20 stockpile (26.5 kt/month in mode 1A at 250 t/h — companion document §4.2) and on the ≈ 16 400 t FeedLime seasonal storage.
